#Abstract
Defect detection methods within manufacturing processes in the era of the Fourth Industrial Revolution have evolved from manual inspection by workers to deep learning-based Automatic Visual Inspection (AVI) systems. However, the performance of these defect detection models relies heavily on high-quality training data; since it is very difficult to obtain defect data in actual manufacturing environments, a serious problem known as data sparsity arises. In this paper, we propose a context-aware steel defect augmentation technique (CASDA) to address these issues. The proposed method establishes the relationship between defects and the background through a matching strategy that reflects the characteristics of both, and expands data diversity by utilizing this information as a condition to generate and synthesize ControlNet-based customized defect images. Through this, we present a novel approach that considers the relationship between defects and the background. Experimental results demon-strate that the proposed method resolves the problem of data sparsity and improves detection performance; it is expected that the augmentation method based on the proposed framework will contribute to the field of industrial defect detection characterized by class imbalance.

#1. Introduction
With the advent of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, defect detection methods in manufacturing processes are rapidly shifting from manual inspection by workers to deep learning-based Automated Visual Inspection (AVI) systems [1]. These systems are demonstrating high performance in various industrial sectors, such as steel, textiles, and semiconductors, and are establishing themselves as a core technology for quality control. However, deep learning-based defect detection models have a limitation in that their performance relies heavily on large-scale, high-quality training data [2].
In real-world industrial environments, securing defect data is an inherently difficult challenge. It is difficult to collect a sufficient amount of data because defects occur infrequently and exhibit diverse shapes, sizes, and patterns [3]. In particular, the scarcity of data regarding minority defects leads to severe class imbalance, which in turn results in a decline in model generalization performance [4]. While existing data augmentation techniques, such as geometric transformations or color variations, can partially improve data diversity, they have limitations in that they fail to reflect the unique structural characteristics of defects and their contextual relationships with the background [5].
To address these issues, data augmentation techniques utilizing generative models have recently been actively researched. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and diffusion-based models have shown promising results in generating defect images [6,7]. However, existing methods fail to adequately reflect the spatial and physical consistency between defects and backgrounds, resulting in issues where the generated images are unrealistic or do not properly reflect the characteristics of actual defects.
The purpose of this study is to propose a context-aware defect data augmentation framework to effectively address the issues of data sparsity and class imbalance that fre-quently occur in industrial settings. In particular, it aims to overcome the limitations of existing data-driven learning by generating realistic defect images that reflect the rela-tionship between the structural characteristics of defects and the background surface.
To this end, this study structures its methodology through the following procedures. First, defect types are systematically classified based on multiple indicators that reflect the morphological and statistical characteristics of the defects. Simultaneously, the surface characteristics of the background areas where defects exist are analyzed and categorized. Subsequently, a compatibility matrix is constructed to quantitatively define the relationship between defects and the background, and this matrix is utilized as a condition to generate contextually consistent defect images using a ControlNet-based generative model. Finally, data diversity is ensured by naturally synthesizing the gener-ated defect images onto normal images.
Ultimately, this study presents a new data augmentation paradigm that considers contextual consistency with the background during the defect generation process, thereby providing a foundation for securing stable and highly reliable defect detection per-formance even in industrial environments with limited data. Furthermore, the proposed method is scalable to various industrial domains and is expected to serve as a practical approach to addressing class imbalance issues.

#2. Related Work
##2.1. Industrial Defect Detection
Deep learning-based industrial defect detection has primarily evolved around su-pervised learning-based object detection or segmentation models [8,9]. Recently, one-class anomaly detection approaches that detect anomalies using only normal data are also being actively researched [10]. While these methods are attracting attention as alternatives considering the limitations of data collection in real industrial environments, they still have limitations in precisely detecting minute defects or various types of defects. In particular, performance degradation of the model is inevitable in situations where suffi-cient defect data is not secured [11].

##2.2. Data Augmentation.
Various data augmentation techniques have been proposed to mitigate the problem of data sparsity. Traditional geometric transformations and color-based augmentation are simple to implement and widely used, but they have limitations in that they fail to reflect the structural characteristics of defects [5]. To address this, cut-paste-based synthesis techniques or methods for recombining defect regions have been proposed, but it is difficult to ensure natural alignment between the background and the defects [12].

##2.3. Generative Models for Augmentation
Recently, there has been an increase in research utilizing GANs and diffusion-based generative models to generate defect images. While these methods offer the advantage of effectively expanding data diversity, they suffer from the problem that the generated defects do not sufficiently reflect the physical characteristics of real industrial environ-ments [4]. In particular, because they do not explicitly consider the spatial and contextual relationships between defects and the background, there is a possibility that unrealistic samples may be generated.

##2.4. Condition-based generation and ControlNet
Research on controlling image generation by utilizing conditional information is also actively underway. In particular, ControlNet is a model capable of precisely con-trolling image generation based on structural conditions and is utilized in various application fields [13]. However, existing research has primarily focused on general image generation or style control, and cases explicitly re-flecting the defect-background relationship in industrial defect data augmentation are limited [4].

#3. Methodology
##3.1. Experimental Setup
In this study, an industrial surface defect dataset was utilized to verify the perfor-mance of the proposed method. The dataset consists of various types of defect classes, each containing distinct morphological characteristics and occurrence patterns [14]. The entire dataset comprises normal images and numerous defect images, characterized by an imbalance in data distribution between classes. In particular, while some defect classes have a sufficient number of samples, a specific class (Class 2) has significantly fewer data, resulting in severe data sparsity. This unbalanced distribution reflects a problem that frequently occurs in real industrial environments and acts as a major factor causing a degradation in detection performance for minority classes. Therefore, this dataset provides a suitable experimental environment for simultaneously evaluating data sparsity and class imbalance issues, and is suitable for verifying the effectiveness of the proposed CASDA framework.

##3.2. CASDA Pipeline
The CASDA (Context-Aware Steel Defect Augmentation) framework proposed in this study consists of a five-stage sequential pipeline designed to simultaneously preserve the geometric characteristics of defects and the physical context of the background. The entire process comprises the stages of defect characteristic analysis, condition generation, context-based synthesis, quality verification, and data integration.

###3.2.1 Stage 1: Geometric ROI Characterization and Suitability Evaluation
First, the Region of Interest (ROI) is extracted from the original dataset, and four geometric indicators are calculated to quantify the morphological characteristics of the defects. 
The linearity index was computed by performing eigenvalue decomposition of the covariance matrix derived from the pixel coordinate set of the defect mask [15].
The solidity of the defect region was defined as the ratio of the actual defect area to the area of its convex hull and was used to quantify defects with irregular boundaries [16].
The area ratio (fill ratio or extent) is defined as the ratio of the defect area to the area of its bounding box and serves as a classical shape descriptor for measuring the rectan-gularity of a region [17].
An ellipse was fitted to the defect region using the least-squares method, and the ratio of the major to minor axis was defined as the aspect ratio [18].

The suitability score for an ROI is calculated by integrating matching quality, spatial continuity, and background stability. 

###3.2.2 Stage 2: Create ControlNet Configuration
A 3-channel hint image and a hybrid prompt are configured to enable the ControlNet model to generate both defect-specific and background-specific features [13]. The 3-channel hint image is designed so that each channel represents different physical characteristics. The R channel reflects the pixel-level geometry of the defect, the G channel reflects surface orientation based on Sobel operations, and the B channel reflects surface roughness through local dispersion [16].
In addition, it creates nodes that combine poor form, background connection, part condition, and surface state modifiers to maintain semantic consistency during the image generation process. Prompts are automatically constructed by concatenating four semantic fields in a fixed template. 
Here, F1 represents the defect type, F2 the background type, F3 the directional texture, and F4 the surface condition that depends on the stability score.
The example of the provided prompt is as follows. If the form of subtype + back-ground is linear_scratch + vertical_stripe and the F4 score is 0.85, it becomes “a high-linearity elongated scratch on vertical striped metal surface with directional texture (pristine condition), steel defect class 2”.

###3.2.3 Stage 3: Context-Based Defect Generation and Synthesis
After extracting background patches from normal images, a composite image is generated by considering the physical alignment between defect characteristics and the background. To this end, a compatibility matrix defining the coexistence probability be-tween defect types and background textures is constructed, and the background is prob-abilistically selected based on this matrix.
Subsequently, the defect images generated using ControlNet are inserted into the selected background, and during the synthesis process, Poisson blending is applied to eliminate discontinuities in boundary regions and ensure visual naturalness.

###3.2.4 Stage 4: Quality Verification Gate
Perform a verification step including the quality of the generated sample. The weighted sum score Q is calculated by evaluating morphological consistency(Q_morph), image sharpness(Q_sharp), the presence of artifacts(Q_artifact), and the area ratio(Q_area).
Finally, by selecting only samples satisfying Q ≥ 0.7 and using them as training data, performance degradation caused by low-quality data is prevented.

###3.2.5 Stage 5: Dataset Integration
Finally, defect masks are encoded for the selected synthetic images and integrated into the existing training dataset. This constructs a final training dataset with improved data diversity and class balance.

##3.3. Experimental Procedure
###3.3.1 Dataset and Data Split
In this study, the Severstal Steel Defect Detection dataset released on Kaggle was used [14]. All images consist of grayscale images with a resolution of 1600 x 256 and reflect the surface defect characteristics of actual industrial environments.
The data was divided into three groups: training, validation, and testing, with a ratio of 70:15:15 applied to each. In the initial experiment, the Dice score (0.6290) was at the same level as when compared to the 80:10:10 split, but the 70:15:15 split was finally adopted because securing approximately 1,000 test samples increased the statistical reli-ability of performance evaluation by class.

###3.3.2 Benchmark Architectures
To verify the universality of the proposed augmentation technique, three models with different structural characteristics were selected. YOLO-MFD has a structure specialized for fine defect detection based on multiscale feature fusion. EB-YOLOv8 is a model that enhances feature representation capabilities by applying BiFPN (Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network). DeepLabV3+ is a representative segmentation-based model that performs precise semantic segmentation at the pixel level, making it suitable for evaluating the precision of defect regions.

###3.3.3 ROI Extraction and Suitability Analysis
A total of 3,247 defect regions (ROIs) were extracted from the training dataset. Each ROI was scored based on the proposed conformity evaluation metric, and the overall average conformity score was found to be 0.68. Based on the distribution of conformity scores, the ROIs were classified into three groups. The High quality group accounted for 61.3% (1,990) of the total and was utilized as high-quality templates. The Acceptable group accounted for 31.5% (1,022) and was utilized restrictively, while the Excluded group accounted for 7.2% (235) and was excluded from the templates.

###3.3.4 Augmentation Statistics and Quality Control
As a result of applying quality validation gates to the 2,500 candidate samples gen-erated by CASDA, 83.0% (2,075 samples) met quality criteria and were adopted as the final training data. 
In particular, for Class 2, where data sparsity is pronounced, 519 samples were added from the existing 247, recording the highest augmentation rate of 110.1%. This ef-fectively mitigated the minority class issue. Overall, a dataset of 7,475 samples was con-structed by adding 2,238 CASDA-generated data samples to the 5,237 original data sam-ples. The proportion of synthetic data was maintained at 42.7% of the total, a design that preserves the distribution characteristics of the actual data while utilizing the effects of the generated data.

#4. Experimental Results and Analysis
##4.1. Performance Evaluation
In the case of YOLO-MFD, CASDA achieved a significant improvement of 2.89 pp at overall mAP@0.5 compared to the Raw dataset. The AP for Class 2, where data sparsity was the most severe, improved by 7.92 pp compared to Raw and a whopping 15.06 pp compared to Copy-Paste, proving the effectiveness of enhanced minority class detection performance. The reason for achieving superior results across all metrics compared to the Copy-Paste method demonstrates that ControlNet-based context-aware generation effectively synthesized defects.
In the case of DeepLabV3+, CASDA analysis revealed that the overall average Dice coefficient value decreased slightly by approximately 0.58 pp compared to the original data. This decrease was attributed to fine boundary artifacts caused by the Poisson blending process applied at the pixel level. This result appears to occur because this de-tection method is much more sensitive to boundary precision than bounding box-based detection methods such as YOLO. Nevertheless, CASDA improved the Class 2 Dice score by 0.69 pp compared to the Copy-Paste method.
We conducted experiments using the YOLO-MFD model to evaluate the impact of each component of the CASDA pipeline on overall performance. When Poisson blending was excluded, the overall mAP decreased by 11.38 pp, showing a particularly sharp de-cline of 23.59 pp in Class 2, a minority class. Additionally, when ROI suitability pruning was not included, the change in overall mAP was minimal (-0.09 pp), but the performance of Class 4 decreased by 2.67 pp. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed CASDA pipeline method. Blending is an essential technique in the image synthesis process, and the results show that high-quality augmentation sets obtained through ROI suitability pruning significantly contribute to improving model performance.

#5. Discussion
In this study, we compared the performance of a benchmark model using an aug-mented dataset obtained through the CASDA framework with that of a conventional da-taset. This approach demonstrates several significant advantages over using only the con-ventional dataset.
First, it is to solve the problem of data sparsity. CASDA contributed to solving the da-ta sparsity problem by improving the AP of Class 2 by 7.92 pp. This is because it effective-ly resolved the 'data sparsity paradox' by extracting detailed defect features and synthesiz-ing them into background-specific feature regions instead of simply duplicating data.
Second, the positive effects of context-based defect generation. Compared to the copy-paste method, CASDA demonstrated a performance advantage of 5.78%p in YOLO-MFD-based mAP and a remarkable 15.06%p in Class 2 AP. While the overall performance dif-ference with the EB-YOLOv8 model was similar, CASDA showed 1.99%p superior per-formance in Class 2. This demonstrates that ControlNet, which learns both defect-specific and background-specific features, is a much more powerful tool for improving defect de-tection performance in minority datasets.
Third, the necessity of Poisson blending. The results of the ablation study showed that omitting the blending step caused a sharp decrease in mAP by 11.38 pp, demonstrat-ing that Poisson blending is a key element of the CASDA pipeline. In other words, this means that even when using high-quality defect patches, performance actually degrades if natural synthesis methods are not used.
While the CASDA framework approach demonstrates several notable advantages, there are some significant limitations and directions for future research.
First, texture structure divergence phenomena appear in the segmentation model. The decrease in the Dice coefficient (-0.58 pp) observed in DeepLabV3+ performance metrics can be explained by this 'texture structure difference' phenomenon. While diffusion mod-els excel at generating defects, they have limitations in restoring precise pixel-level bound-aries during the synthesis process. In particular, Poisson blending guarantees a certain degree of realism but cannot eliminate all fine boundary artifacts. These artifacts act like noise during the training and detection processes of the segmentation model, degrading accuracy.
To overcome these limitations, more sophisticated blending and post-processing techniques must be introduced. To improve the statistical consistency between defects and the background, multi-scale blending and texture-aware compositing techniques must be applied. In addition, we plan to enhance visual naturalness through normalization and brightness correction techniques that take lighting conditions into account. Afterwards, you should also consider applying GAN or diffusion model-based post-processing tech-niques to improve the quality of the synthetic images.
Finally, research must be conducted to establish a universal pipeline applicable to various fields. We must conduct research to establish an adaptive pipeline capable of con-tributing to performance improvement across various industrial sectors, including not on-ly the steel defects addressed in this study but also diverse components such as PCBs, wood, and carpets. Subsequently, research comparing performance through various benchmark models must be conducted to verify whether general-purpose pipelines can contribute to improving data sparsity in real-world applications.

#6. Conclusion
This study proposes the CASDA method. CASDA utilizes image data of defects on steel surfaces to classify defect types and the characteristics of the background where the defects are located. Next, we generate customized defect images using ControlNet. We present a data augmentation method by synthesizing the generated defect images. Ex-perimental results show that the proposed method solves the data sparsity problem and improves detection performance. First, the CASDA framework achieved a high quality validation pass rate of 83.0% and, in particular, increased the number of the rarest Class 2 training samples by 110.1%. Secondly, the CASDA framework confirmed that mAP performance was consistently improved through three benchmark models, including YOLO-MFD and EB-YOLOv8. Finally, through ablation studies, we confirmed that Pois-son blending is an essential element in maintaining detection performance by eliminating boundary discontinuities in synthetic images. Consequently, the proposed framework-based data augmentation method is expected to contribute to the field of industrial defect detection characterized by class imbalance.
Future research aims to improve the reliability of augmented datasets by introducing more sophisticated data blending and post-processing techniques. Additionally, we plan to conduct research that contributes to enhancing the performance of defect detection models across various industries by constructing a general-purpose pipeline applicable to diverse sectors.
